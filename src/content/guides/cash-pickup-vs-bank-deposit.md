## Which payout method costs the least?

The way the recipient gets the money is part of the price. The same provider can quote one rate and fee for a bank deposit, another for a mobile wallet and another for cash collected at an agent. Cash is the most expensive to deliver: someone has to hold notes at a counter, staff it and insure it.

The World Bank tracks this across thousands of quotes. In its Remittance Prices Worldwide report for the third quarter of 2025, the global average total cost of sending the equivalent of 200 dollars was:

| Payout method | Average cost (Q3 2025) |
|---|---|
| Mobile wallet | 3.92% |
| Cash payout | 5.56% |
| Bank account | 7.86% |

*Source: World Bank, Remittance Prices Worldwide, Issue 54. Averages across all services surveyed, including banks.*

The bank-account average looks high because it includes banks, which are usually the most expensive way to send small amounts. When you exclude banks and compare specialist services, bank deposit is often among the cheapest options. In RateRunner's snapshot of 15 October 2026, the best quotes for 1,000 dollars from the US to Mexico, India and the Philippines cost about 1% or less (leaving aside promotional rates), while the costliest quotes in the same corridors cost 3.6% to 5.6%. You can see current quotes in the [corridor ledgers](/send/).

The useful rule: compare the specific payout method the recipient will use. A cheap headline quote for a bank deposit tells you little about the price of cash pickup from the same service.

## When does cash pickup make sense?

Cash pickup exists for good reasons:

- **The recipient has no bank or wallet account**, or their account cannot receive international payments.
- **Money is needed today in physical form**, for example in an emergency where digital payments are not accepted locally.
- **The recipient lives far from a bank branch** but close to a pawnshop, supermarket or post office that acts as an agent.

In those cases, the extra cost buys access. It is worth checking whether the recipient could open a mobile wallet instead. In many corridors, including the Philippines, wallets now combine low cost with speed. The [Philippines guide](/guides/send-money-to-philippines/) compares the options there.

## How do speed and convenience compare?

| | Cash pickup | Bank deposit | Mobile wallet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical speed | Often minutes once funded | Minutes on instant rails; next business day otherwise | Often minutes |
| Recipient needs | ID and reference number, a trip to an agent | A bank account in their name | A verified wallet in their name |
| Opening hours | Agent's hours | Depends on local clearing system | Usually 24/7 |
| Record of payment | Receipt at the counter | Bank statement | Wallet history |

Speeds are typical patterns, not guarantees. See [how long transfers take](/guides/how-long-international-transfers-take/) for what decides the timing.

## Is cash pickup riskier?

For honest transfers between people who know each other, cash pickup is safe when the details are right. The risk sits elsewhere: cash pickup is the payout method fraudsters prefer, because once cash is collected it is very hard to trace or recover.

Treat these as warning signs:

- Someone you have never met in person asks you to send money for pickup.
- You are told to send money to claim a prize, release a parcel, pay a "fee" for a job or loan, or help with an emergency you cannot verify.
- You are asked to lie to the agent about the purpose of the payment.

Transfers to a bank account or wallet are not immune to fraud, but they leave a clearer trail. In the US, the CFPB explains your cancellation and error-resolution rights for remittances; cancellation only works before the money is collected.

## What does paying in cash cost the sender?

Since 1 January 2026, US senders who fund a remittance with cash, a money order or a cashier's check also pay a 1% federal excise tax under 26 U.S.C. 4475. Paying from a bank account or with a US-issued card is exempt. This is separate from how the recipient is paid: the tax depends on how you pay, not on cash pickup. The [US remittance tax guide](/guides/us-remittance-transfer-tax/) has the details.

## Getting the details right

Most failed cash collections come from small mismatches:

1. **Use the recipient's full legal name** exactly as it appears on their ID, in the same order.
2. **Share the reference number privately** with the recipient, and only with them.
3. **Check the pickup location and hours** before the recipient travels.
4. **Confirm the payout currency.** Some locations pay only local currency; others offer US dollars at a different rate.

For bank and wallet deposits, the same principle applies to account names and numbers. Then compare the full cost for the payout type you chose in the [calculator](/).
